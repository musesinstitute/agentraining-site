## Story
A fictional advisor struggles with a common objection: "Your premium is too high." A manager sees the actual practice evidence available in the demo, makes a coaching decision, and examines follow-up practice. **Real product UI, fictional demo data.** Never imply that the simulated advisor is a real client or that the product has verified retention/production gains.

## Must-pass filming feasibility gates
1. Simulator: verify a stable, repeatable "premium too high" scenario and a coherent weak response; record only test/fictional data. If voice transcription is unreliable, use existing text entry and show it honestly.
2. Mission Control: confirm the precise demo persona and evidence that can be inspected. A fictional scenario is fine, but no invented source count or record.
3. Manager action: distinguish a clickable *simulated* coaching decision from an actual saved assignment. Show the label clearly.
4. Second practice: confirm whether an actual new practice can be completed and shown in the manager UI. If not, use a clearly labeled illustrated reassessment and change narration accordingly.
5. Browser: capture fresh-session production walkthrough with no broken navigation, login walls or personal data.
6. End card: verify where the 14-day pilot CTA goes before export.

## Editorial direction
- One story only; avoid saying "Four Studios", "Three Phases", "Seven Steps" in narration.
- Actual screen footage must dominate (at least 65 of 90 seconds); no 15-second static cards.
- Use 2–5 second cuts and cursor zoom/callouts for the important evidence; never fabricate UI controls or modify screenshots to imply features.
- Add English narration and synchronized captions. 16:9, 1920x1080 target; export MP4 H.264.
- Keep disclaimer readable: "Actual product interface; fictional demonstration scenario and data."
- Use no real client details, no fabricated results, no unverified ROI or retention claim.

## Minimal acceptance review
